I actually think your proposed flow is stronger than trying to recreate an entire commercial ESG rating methodology.

The judges will almost certainly know that **S&P, MSCI, Sustainalytics, Moody's, etc. do not publish their complete scoring algorithms.** If you claim to reproduce them exactly, they'll challenge you.

Instead, position it as:

> **"An AI-powered ESG Assessment Engine inspired by the methodology patterns used by major ESG rating agencies (S&P CSA, CDP, ISSB, TNFD, SCB Climate Risk Questionnaire), rather than an attempt to replicate proprietary models."**

That is technically accurate and much easier to defend.

One refinement I'd make for the scoring logic is to avoid presenting a single **58/100** without context. Instead, expose the score composition:

| Component                | Status   | Score | Weight |
| ------------------------ | -------- | ----: | -----: |
| Climate Disclosure       | Complete |    90 |    25% |
| Biodiversity             | Partial  |    45 |    20% |
| Physical Risk Exposure   | Missing  |   N/A |    30% |
| Physical Risk Adaptation | Strong   |    80 |    25% |

Then report:

* **Calculated Score (available evidence):** 74/100
* **Coverage:** 70% of weighted criteria evaluated
* **Confidence:** High (or a numeric confidence score)
* **Data Gaps:** Physical risk exposure data unavailable

This separates *poor performance* from *insufficient evidence*, which is much more defensible and aligns better with how professional ESG assessments communicate uncertainty.
