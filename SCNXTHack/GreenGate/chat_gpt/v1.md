This is actually **the most important question** to answer before building the solution.

You're not building an ESG score **for the sake of having a score**. You're building a tool that helps the bank make **better green finance decisions**.

Let's think from the perspective of a bank.

---

# Today's Process (Without AI)

A company comes to your bank and says:

> "We need ₹500 crore to build a new township."

The bank asks for:

* Financial statements
* Sustainability report
* Climate disclosures
* ESG questionnaires
* Risk questionnaires
* Compliance documents

Someone in the bank (ESG analyst or Risk analyst) spends days or weeks reading hundreds of pages.

They answer questions like:

* Does this company have a climate strategy?
* Is biodiversity considered?
* Is the site exposed to floods?
* Are adaptation plans in place?
* Does this investment qualify as green finance?

Only then do they recommend whether to lend.

---

# Who uploads the documents?

There are two common models.

### Option 1 — Relationship Manager (Most likely for the hackathon)

The bank employee handling the client uploads:

* Sustainability Report
* Annual Report
* CRQ questionnaire
* Optional project documents

The AI analyzes everything.

This is the simplest and most realistic demo.

---

### Option 2 — Corporate Customer Portal

The company uploads the documents through an online portal.

The AI generates a preliminary ESG assessment before a human reviews it.

This is how many banks are moving toward digital onboarding.

---

# Who checks the scores?

Not the customer.

The score is primarily for **internal bank teams**, such as:

* Credit Risk
* ESG Risk
* Sustainability Team
* Green Finance Team
* Credit Committee

Think of it like a **credit score**.

Customers don't decide their credit score.

Banks use it internally to guide lending decisions.

---

# How does the score benefit the bank?

This is the key message for the hackathon.

The score helps answer:

> **"Should we finance this company or project as a green investment?"**

Examples:

### Company A

Strong climate governance

Flood resilience

Science-based targets

Biodiversity assessment

Score:

```text
82/100
```

Decision:

✅ Eligible for Green Loan

---

### Company B

No biodiversity study

No emissions targets

Score:

```text
42/100
```

Decision:

❌ Not eligible yet

or

Approve only after improvements.

---

# Even better: Think beyond "Approve/Reject"

Banks rarely reject solely on ESG.

Instead:

**Score → Risk → Decision**

Example:

| ESG Score | Interpretation      | Possible Bank Action                                                                                      |
| --------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 80–100    | Strong ESG maturity | Eligible for green finance or preferential terms                                                          |
| 60–79     | Moderate            | Finance with monitoring or improvement commitments                                                        |
| Below 60  | Significant gaps    | Request additional disclosures, mitigation plans, or classify as a standard loan rather than a green loan |

This aligns much better with how lending decisions are made.

# Even more valuable: Consistency

Different analysts may score the same company differently.

Example:

Analyst A

> "Looks good."

Score = 78

Analyst B

> "Missing biodiversity assessment."

Score = 63

AI applies the same evaluation rules every time, improving consistency across assessments.

---

# The Green Finance angle

This is where your hackathon theme comes in.

The objective isn't to create another ESG dashboard.

The objective is to help the bank answer:

> **"Can we confidently classify this financing as green or sustainability-linked?"**

Your system helps by:

* Extracting ESG evidence from company disclosures.
* Identifying missing information against the bank's questionnaire.
* Assessing alignment with the bank's ESG criteria.
* Highlighting climate and nature-related risks.
* Producing an explainable recommendation for analysts.
