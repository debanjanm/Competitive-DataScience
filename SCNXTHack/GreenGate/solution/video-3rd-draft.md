# GreenGate — Demo Video Script (Third Draft)

**Hard limit: 4 minutes, 200 MB** (per the submission guide).
**This script runs about 3 minutes 40 seconds.**

Written in simple English. Short sentences. Read at a calm pace, roughly two and
a half words per second. Pause at each full stop.

## Full narration

### 0:00–0:14 — The problem

*On screen: title card, or a stack of documents.*

> A company asks the bank for a green loan. To answer, an ESG analyst reads
> hundreds of pages - annual reports, sustainability reports. It takes days.
> And two analysts often give two different scores.

---

### 0:14–0:28 — What GreenGate is

*On screen: product name.*

> This is GreenGate. It reads those documents, answers the bank's own ESG
> questionnaire, scores the company, and recommends how to structure the loan.
> Every answer is backed by a line from the document.

---

### 0:28–0:45 — What we do not claim

*On screen: positioning slide, or hold on the product name.*

> One point before we start. We are not copying S&P or MSCI. Their models are
> private. We follow the same ideas public frameworks use - TCFD, ISSB, TNFD -
> inside the bank's own questionnaire. And the score is plain maths, not AI.

*Say this early. It stops a judge raising it later as a challenge.*

---

### 0:45–1:01 — The input

*On screen: the upload form. Point at each field as you name it.*

> Let's screen a company. The input is simple. The company name - ABC Green
> Infrastructure. The deal documents - we upload the annual report and the
> sustainability report. And an optional comment, where the relationship manager
> adds context. Then submit.

---

### 1:01–1:31 — While it processes: the six steps

*On screen: the progress dialog, running in real time. The staged messages
appear one by one - "Extracting document content", "Detecting
sustainability-relevant sections", "Classifying applicable scoring framework",
"Synthesizing eligibility rationale". Do not cut or speed up this clip.*

> While it runs, here is what is happening. Step one takes in the company and
> the files. Step two reads both documents and pulls out only the facts that are
> actually stated - no guessing. Step three answers each question in the bank's
> questionnaire, using only those facts. Step four does the scoring maths in
> plain Python. Step five writes the recommendation. Step six sets the final
> verdict. About thirty seconds, start to finish.

*74 words, paced to fill the whole 30-second wait. This turns loading time into
the architecture explanation, so no screen time is wasted. If the run finishes
early, hold on the result header until you reach the last line.*

---

### 1:31–1:56 — The scores

*On screen: result header. Point at each number in turn.*

> Here is the result. At the top, the verdict. Then the overall score, out of
> one hundred. Next to it, the disclosure rate - how much of the questionnaire
> the company actually answered. That matters. A low score can mean poor
> performance, or it can mean they simply did not disclose. Those are different
> problems. Below, the three pillar scores.

*This is the strongest single idea in the demo. Do not rush the two sentences
about disclosure rate.*

---

### 1:56–2:06 — Executive summary

*On screen: the summary paragraph under the scores.*

> Under the scores, the executive summary. Two or three lines on what kind of
> company this is - where it is strong, where it is thin.

---

### 2:06–2:26 — Assessment tab

*On screen: click Assessment. Scroll slowly through the three sections.*

> Then three tabs. First, Assessment - the analyst's tab. Strengths, what the
> company clearly does well. Disclosure gaps, what is missing or only half
> answered. We say gaps, not failures, because usually the company simply did
> not report it. And recommended actions, the steps that would close those gaps.

---

### 2:26–2:46 — Financing view tab

*On screen: click Financing view. Pause on each of the four sections.*

> Second, the Financing view - the credit committee's tab. Eligibility rationale
> explains why this company landed in this band and not the one above. Financing
> recommendation gives the deal structure. Conditions and covenants list what the
> bank should demand before lending. Monitoring KPIs list what to track
> afterwards.

---

### 2:46–3:13 — Evidence tab

*On screen: click Evidence. Scroll the table. Zoom on one quoted line.*

> Third, Evidence - for the analyst, and for audit. This one matters most. Every
> question, its status, its weight, and the exact sentence from the report that
> supports it, with the document it came from. Nothing is invented. If the report
> does not say it, we mark it Not Disclosed. A bank cannot lend on a number it
> cannot explain. This tab is that explanation.

*Land the last two lines firmly. This is the differentiator.*

---

### 3:13–3:27 — Download the report

*On screen: click "Download summary report". Show the file landing, and if time
allows, open it for one second.*

> One more thing. Download summary report saves everything you just saw - the
> scores, the gaps, the recommendation, and all the cited evidence - in one file.
> That is what goes into the credit file.

*Keep it brisk. The point is that the evidence travels with the score, so the
audit trail survives outside the tool.*

---

### 3:27–3:40 — Closing

*On screen: the comparison strip with all four companies.*

> Four companies, screened the same way, landing in all four bands. Same
> questions, same rules, every time. The analyst stays in control. GreenGate does
> the reading, and shows its work. Thank you.

## Audience map (for reference while recording)

The narration names the audience as each tab opens, so there is no separate
"who reads what" section. This table is the full picture.

| Screen area | Who reads it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Verdict, overall score, disclosure rate, pillar scores | Credit committee | The decision, at a glance |
| Executive summary | Both | Two lines on what the company is |
| Assessment tab | ESG analyst | The gaps to chase, the actions to request |
| Financing view tab | Credit committee | Deal structure, covenants, what to monitor |
| Evidence tab | ESG analyst, and audit | Proof behind every status |
| Downloaded report | Both, plus the credit file | The record that outlives the tool |
